Goal

After go-live the HRMS is the only system of record. Every candidate, offer, joining, leave, transfer, bed allocation, salary change and exit is created here. The Realtime India portal is only the source of machine swipes.

The management question it must answer at any moment: "What does this property, this cluster, this city or the whole company cost us this month, and how does that compare with budget?" That cost includes salary, employer contributions and the costs that never appear in anyone's CTC, such as accommodation.

1 · Hiring lifecycle: candidate › Joining ID › Employee ID

Every state in the lifecycle is listed below. Each change is dated, has a reason and is kept on the person's timeline, so HR can always see where someone is and why.

What gets built

  • Offer without an ID. The OI- number is issued only on acceptance. Before that, the offer is a numbered version under the candidate (SI-0042 offer v2).
  • HR records the candidate's answer (no candidate link, decided 16 Nov): Accepted, Question about the breakup (what was asked; HR revises and sends a new version) or Declined (reason). Every answer is dated and kept on the timeline.
  • Offer expiry, with reminders before it lapses.
  • No-show waiting window (HR decides). When someone doesn't come on the joining date, the case goes to "Waiting", not closed. HR sets the window in settings (default 7 days) and can extend it for one person, with a reason. The case shows "day 3 of 7". HR gets a reminder each day and can log call attempts. If the person comes within the window, HR marks them joined on the actual date and the process resumes from where it stopped: the same offer, documents and link, no re-entry. When the window ends, HR confirms and the case closes as No-show; HR can choose automatic close in settings.
  • Joining-day outcomes: joined, no-show, declined on the day, date moved. Each one closes or moves the case with a reason, which feeds a drop-off report (where candidates are lost, by recruiter, property and role).
  • Joining ID record from day 1. The person can swipe and be paid before getting an Employee ID. Their onboarding checklist adds a background check step (a vendor or manual result plus an uploaded report; it can block approval).
  • Early exit. If a person says they are quitting in the first days, the case closes as "Early exit" straight away. If they just stop coming, the case goes to Waiting with the same HR window as a no-show (same setting, can be extended per person, day counter, call log). If they come back within the window, the process resumes, and the missed days are marked absent. When the window ends, the case closes as "Early exit". They are paid for the days worked, with no full separation case.
  • Employee ID after approval. When every step is approved, the EMP- ID is issued. The Joining ID stays as an alias, so searches and old documents still work.
  • Contract staff without an Employee ID. Settings per employment type:
    • "issues an Employee ID: yes/no";
    • "leave: none / only some types / all";
    • its own ID series.
    Contract staff keep their own series (e.g. CW-0001) for life. They are paid by company payroll like everyone else, with attendance and cost (decided 16 Nov).
  • Status timeline on every person: one list of every change (who, when, why). The statuses are the ones in the diagram above.
Technical note: today the Employee ID is the record's key everywhere (punches, payroll rows, leave). Switching from Joining ID to Employee ID needs a careful one-time "re-key" of that person's records in one transaction. In the first days that's only a handful of records, so it is safe. A Joining-ID person is a normal employee who simply isn't fully approved yet: they are paid through normal payroll (decided 16 Nov). The background check is recorded by HR (result plus uploaded report). A setting decides whether a failed or pending check blocks the Employee ID; it is on by default.

2 · Access: admin can give anyone anything

  • Admin has every right, always. This is locked, and only another admin can remove it.
  • Rights for one person on top of their roles: grant a single right (e.g. "View salary") or block one, with an optional end date. These show on the Access page and on the employee page.
  • Scope by state, city, cluster or property, not only by a list of locations. A new property added to a cluster is covered automatically.
  • Role change from the employee page (admin, or anyone given the right), with an audit trail.
  • "Who can see what" report: for any person, their effective rights and where each came from (role or personal).

3 · Pay structure: fixed and variable, by amount or percentage

  • Component designer (admin). Each component is Fixed or Variable, earning or deduction, with a calculation method:
    • fixed ₹ amount;
    • % of CTC, of gross, of Basic, or of another component;
    • "the remainder" (balancing figure);
    • a formula with a min/max cap.
    Also set per component: taxable, PF wage, ESI wage.
  • Structures are made of components. Example: CTC ₹35,000 = Fixed ₹20,000 (Basic 50% of fixed, HRA 40% of Basic, the rest special allowance) + Variable ₹15,000.
  • Variable rules per component: payout cycle (monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, yearly) and what it depends on: a manual amount entered by HR (decided 17 Nov; attendance %, manager rating, targets or a manual amount), plus whether it's paid pro-rata on exit.
  • Enter by CTC or by gross. The system works out the rest and shows the same breakup the candidate sees on the offer.
  • Compliance check: warns when the wage definition under the new labour codes (Basic + DA at least 50% of total pay) isn't met.
  • No break for current employees: today's percentage structures are converted into templates that give the same result. Payroll is compared before and after on a copy of your data before switching.

4 · Accommodation: flats, leases, beds and the true cost

A new page, Settings › Accommodation:

  • Units (flat, house or PG). Fields: address, cluster, nearest properties, gender, beds, landlord and contact.
  • Lease per unit: start date, end date or "ongoing", notice period, monthly rent, deposit, maintenance, yearly increase, and documents (agreement, ID). Reminders before the lease ends or the rent increases.
  • Monthly running cost: rent + electricity + water + Wi-Fi + housekeeping (fixed or entered each month).
  • Allocation: when someone joins or transfers into a city, the system lists units in their cluster with free beds, nearest first, matching gender (and shift if wanted). HR clicks Assign. Check-in and check-out dates are kept for each person, with a history of every stay.
  • Cost split (company cost, not in CTC), decided 17 Nov. The unit's month cost is divided equally among the people who stayed there that month: ₹12,000 with 1 person = ₹12,000; 2 people = ₹6,000 each; 4 = ₹3,000 each. A day-by-day split is kept as an option for later, off by default. A month with nobody in the unit counts as empty-unit cost for its property.
  • Not on the salary sheet (decided 17 Nov). Company accommodation is not part of payroll at all. It never appears on the payslip or salary register and counts for nothing statutory. It is used only to calculate the company's cost.
  • Minimum wage top-up stays as it is. The meal/accommodation amount HR enters today (e.g. ₹18k salary topped up by ₹2k to meet a ₹20k state minimum wage, then deducted) keeps working. It is a separate line from the company accommodation above.
  • Occupancy board: beds free, filled and by gender, per cluster and city, plus leases ending soon.

5 · Policy conflict check (decided 16 Nov)

Today the system accepts two overlapping policies silently. Example: a 6-day policy is already on a department, and a 5.5-day policy is then put on a location. Both are saved, and the employees caught in both get whichever one ranks higher, without anyone being told.

  • When a policy is assigned, the system first checks who it would cover. If any of those people already get a different policy, it stops and shows: "6-day policy is already applied here (through the Operations department) to 42 people."
  • HR then chooses:
    • Replace for those 42 (they move to the new policy from a chosen date);
    • Keep the existing one for them (the new policy covers only the rest);
    • Pick people one by one;
    • Cancel.
  • The choice is saved as explicit exceptions, so the result never depends on a hidden ranking.
  • Effective policy view on each employee: which policy they get and why.
  • Overlap report: lists every person currently covered by two or more policies today, so the existing overlaps can be cleaned up before go-live.
  • Company policies to acknowledge can also target a cluster, city or named employees.

7 · Punch machines: the cluster is one pool (decided 16 Nov)

Each machine holds about 1,000 people. The machines of one cluster work as one shared store: a cluster with 10 properties (10 machines) holds about 10,000 people. An employee can punch at any machine in their cluster, and the record is collected later. That is why moving someone between properties inside a cluster needs no re-enrolment. Moving them to another cluster does.

  • A capacity per machine. A cluster's capacity is the total of its machines, and the board shows people against capacity for each cluster. It warns before a cluster is full, so that joiners can't be onboarded into a full cluster without a warning.
  • Where each record is stored: the employee's own property's machine first. If that machine is full, the next machine in the cluster with free space.
  • Transfers: a move inside the cluster needs no machine task. A move to another cluster creates a to-do: "Enrol on cluster B, remove from cluster A".
  • Joiners and exits create the same kind of to-do (enrol, remove).
  • Punches from any machine in the employee's cluster count. A punch from a machine in another cluster is flagged for HR.
  • The HRMS does not press Add/Delete on the portal (that stays a manual step for the portal operator). It produces the to-do list and checks the result in the portal's employee list.
